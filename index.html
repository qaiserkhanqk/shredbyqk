      Fitness Age

      How old is your body, really?

      Built on the HUNT Study, which measured the fitness of over 4,600 healthy adults. Three things you know about yourself give your fitness, and your Fitness Age is the age whose average matches it.

      Add your age and sex under Today → Profile first.

      150 minutes of training or brisk activity a week?

      Extra checks: push-ups and sit-and-rise

      Sit down cross-legged on the floor and stand back up, without leaning on anything if you can. Start at 10: take off 1 for each hand, knee or forearm you use (going down and coming up), and 0.5 for a wobble.

      The research behind it

      Your fitness: the HUNT Study fitness model (Nes, Nauman and colleagues, Norwegian University of Science and Technology) estimates VO2peak, the gold-standard fitness measure, from age, waist, resting heart rate and activity. It was built and checked against lab treadmill tests.

      Your Fitness Age: compared with lab-measured VO2max in 4,631 healthy adults aged 20–90 (Aspenes et al. 2011, PLOS ONE).

      Why it matters: in 37,000+ adults followed for over 20 years, each step up in this fitness (1 MET) went with about 15% lower risk of dying early (Nes et al. 2014, Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise).

      Extra checks: push-ups (Yang et al. 2019, JAMA Network Open), sit-and-rise (Brito et al. 2012, European Journal of Preventive Cardiology) and resting heart rate (Zhang et al. 2016, CMAJ).

      Body check

      How's your system today?

      Uses only your phone's camera and screen. Readings are compared with your own normal, never with other people.

      Heart rate · 15 seconds

      Fingertip pulse

      Recovery · 1 minute, each morning

      Heart-rate variability (HRV)

      Higher than your normal usually means well recovered; lower can mean poor sleep, stress, illness or hard training. Measure it at the same time each morning, sitting, before coffee.

      Alertness · 3 minutes

      Reaction-time test

      The standard sleep-loss test used in research (PVT-B). Tap as fast as you can each time the numbers start counting.

      Check it against a chest strap

      To find out how accurate your phone is, and correct it: wear a chest strap (for example a Polar H10 with its free app) and take a Shred reading at the same moment. After each reading, type in the strap's number.

      How accurate is this?

      Heart rate: fingertip camera readings are usually within 2–5 beats a minute of a hospital ECG. Less accurate if your heartbeat is irregular.

      HRV: tracks your changes well but reads a little high, especially when HRV is low. So Shred only compares it with your own readings, after at least 5 mornings.

      Alertness: the 3-minute phone test is validated against the 10-minute lab version. Phone screens add their own delay, so always use the same phone.

      None of these diagnose anything. If your resting heart rate is often above 100 or below 40 without a reason (such as being very fit), or you feel your heart racing or skipping, see a doctor.

      Sukoon

      Calm your mind

      Or pick what you're feeling. You'll breathe slowly, listen to a short recitation, then make a dua from the Sunnah.

      Settings
      Your sessions
      Why it works, and what's not proven

      Slow breathing at about 6 breaths a minute switches on the body's rest system and raises heart-rate variability. It has the strongest evidence here.

      Listening to the Quran lowered anxiety in most of the 28 trials in a 2018 systematic review, including before surgery, chemotherapy and MRI scans. “Verily, in the remembrance of Allah do hearts find rest.” (13:28)

      Binaural tones reduced anxiety with a medium effect in meta-analyses. The tones stop before the recitation, so the Quran is listened to in silence (7:204).

      Not proven: no verse or hadith links a frequency to a surah, and claims about 432 Hz, 528 Hz and 7.83 Hz rest on small pilot studies. The surahs are matched to each goal by their meaning.

      Sukoon is a wellbeing exercise, not treatment for a mental-health condition.

      Movement tests

      Test how you move

      1. Treadmill is ideal. Phone at hip height, side-on, about 3 metres away, zoomed in slightly.
      2. Wear shorts or leggings so knees and ankles are visible.
      3. Walk naturally for 10 seconds. Then film facing the camera to check hip drop and knees.
      Countermovement jump

      Measures jump height from your time in the air, the method jump mats use. Track it to see power and day-to-day readiness.

      1. Phone on the floor or a low step, side-on, 2–3 metres away. Your feet must stay in view.
      2. Hands on hips. Stand still for a second, dip, and jump straight up. Land on the same spot with straight legs.
      3. Do 3 jumps with a short rest. For the most precise result, film in slow-motion and upload it.
      Single-leg squat knee test

      Measures how far your knee drifts inward on one leg (the frontal plane projection angle), a screen clinics use for knee-injury risk.

      1. Face the camera from 2–3 metres, phone at knee height. Whole legs in view, shorts or leggings.
      2. Stand on your left leg, other foot lifted. Do 5 slow squats to a comfortable depth.
      3. QK tells you when to switch. Then 5 on your right leg. 30 seconds in total.
      Landing test

      Measures how far each knee caves inward when you land, the ACL-injury risk sign footballers, hockey and racket players are screened for. Compares left and right.

      1. Face the camera from 2–3 metres, phone at knee height, whole legs in view.
      2. Stand on a low box or step (about 30 cm). Step off and land on both feet, then stand tall.
      3. Do 3 landings in 12 seconds. Slow-motion video gives the most precise result.
      Bowling action check

      Side-on: front-knee angle at front-foot contact and release (braced or collapsing). From behind: trunk side-bend at release, a back-injury risk factor in young fast bowlers.

      1. Film in slow motion (120–240 fps) and upload it. Normal video is too slow to catch release.
      2. Side-on: camera at hip height level with the crease, 8–10 m away, whole body in frame through the stride.
      3. From behind: camera behind the bowling crease, in line with the stumps.
