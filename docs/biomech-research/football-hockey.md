# Football (soccer) and field hockey: biomechanics evidence for a phone-camera lab

Researched 28 November 2026 using PubMed and web search. Every paper below was retrieved and its abstract (or full text, where noted) read. Where a number could not be checked against a source, it says **unverified**. Nothing here is a medical claim. These are screening and coaching markers, not diagnoses (see decision D-013 on device status).

**How to read the columns**
- **Camera view**: S = 2D side-on (sagittal), F = 2D front-on (frontal), 3D = MediaPipe world landmarks from one phone. "S (240 fps)" means the published method needed slow motion.
- **Evidence** labels: **study-backed** = a peer-reviewed study links the marker or the fix to the outcome; **coaching practice** = widely used, but no study found that tests it directly.
- **Confidence**: High = prospective or meta-analytic evidence; Medium = cross-sectional lab studies that agree with each other; Low = single small study, expert framework or coaching practice only.
- "No validated cutoff" means the studies report associations or group means, not a pass/fail threshold. Any threshold the app uses there is a product decision and must be labelled as such.

**Hamstring fixes, with evidence level**
- Nordic hamstring exercise: programmes that include it roughly halve hamstring injuries (risk ratio 0.49, van Dyk 2019, 8,459 athletes; IRR 0.49 in soccer, Al Attar 2017). **Study-backed, High.**
- Sprint exposure itself: 4 weeks of sprint training raised eccentric hamstring torque about as much as Nordics (Sancese 2023, n = 18). **Study-backed, Low.**
- Lengthening-type rehab (Askling L-protocol): return to training in 28 vs 51 days (Askling 2013 RCT, n = 75). **Study-backed, High.** This is rehab, not screening, and sits in clinician territory.
- Hip-extension work (RDL, single-leg bridge): already logged in learnings (Breed 2026). **Study-backed.**

| Lateral foot-plant width | Cut | F | Narrower plant, foot closer to neutral, midfoot/forefoot contact (Donelon 2020) | No validated cutoff | Wide plant raises hip abduction and knee load | Technique | Cone cutting drills with foot-placement cue | Study-backed (framework) | Medium |
| Approach speed | Cut | S | Faster approach = faster cut but higher KAM ("performance-injury conflict"; Dos'Santos 2021; McBurnie 2021) | Context-dependent | Both performance and knee load rise with speed | Braking strength | Progress approach speed only once technique holds | Study-backed | Medium |

**Programme-level fix**: FIFA 11+ reduced overall injuries (IRR 0.61, 4 cluster RCTs; Thorborg 2017) and knee/ACL injuries in male college soccer (Silvers-Granelli 2017). It did **not** reduce knee load in cutting in biomechanics studies (Dos'Santos 2019 scoping review). So it protects players without visibly changing the cut. **Study-backed, High.**

**Bottom line for the app**: the strongest phone-measurable markers are (1) sprint times and F-V profile, (2) a S-MAS-style side-on sprint score at 240 fps, (3) front-on landing FPPA or knee-ankle separation ratio, and (4) LESS-style landing checklists. Cutting frontal-plane angles and anything rotational (pelvis rotation, lumbar rotation in the drag flick) are weak from one camera. They should be shown as "movement-quality flags", not numbers.

## Gaps and cautions

- **No validated pass/fail cutoffs** exist for most sprint, cutting, kicking, goalkeeper and hockey markers. Only these have published cutoffs: LESS ≥5 (one cohort, contradicted by another), hip abduction ≤35.4% BW and external rotation ≤20.3% BW (handheld dynamometer, not camera), S-MAS per-point risk, and the 13% horizontal-force asymmetry (treadmill).
- **Elite degree values** for first-step shin angle, first-step trunk angle, max-velocity touch-down distance and Mann's front-side angles were not found in a verifiable source: **unverified**.
- **Field hockey** injury evidence is thin. The low-posture link to back pain is a loading finding, not an injury finding, and one incidence study found no excess back pain.
- Several key studies are small (n = 6 to 14). Bramah 2026 (n = 126) and Hewett 2005 (n = 205) are the only large prospective biomechanics studies here.
- Hewett 2005 used 3D lab capture in female athletes. Its 8° and 2.5× figures do not transfer directly to a phone measurement.
- Any public claim about the app's accuracy is blocked by D-013 and the unrun camera-validation study.
