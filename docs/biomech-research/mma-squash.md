# Biomechanics evidence: MMA / combat sports and squash

For the phone-camera "biomechanics lab". Compiled 2026-11-28 from PubMed (searched via the PubMed tool) and web search. Every paper below was checked on PubMed unless it is marked otherwise. A number is quoted only if it appeared in an abstract I read. Anything else is marked **unverified**.

**How to read the Evidence column:** "study-backed" means a peer-reviewed study measured it. "coaching practice" means common coaching teaching with no study found. "analogue" means the evidence comes from a related sport (usually badminton), not the one named.

**Big caveat for the app:** almost none of these thresholds come from studies that tie a fault to injury in prospective data. Most are elite-vs-less-skilled comparisons (cross-sectional, small samples, often n = 8–20). Treat every "fault threshold" as a coaching flag, not a diagnosis. The compliance rules in CLAUDE.md and decision D-013 (camera accuracy claims are blocked) apply.

**Squash injury context (study-backed):**
- England Squash pros 2004–2015: 76.48% of injuries were lower limb. Ankle/heel 20.81%, thigh 12.69%, knee 10.83%, hip/groin 9.48%, lower leg 7.61%, foot 7.61%, buttock 7.45%. Mostly soft tissue (Horsley 2020).
- Meta-analysis: pooled 2.01 injuries per 1,000 athlete-days. Heterogeneity was extreme (I² = 99.65%), so the rate is unreliable. Lower-limb soft tissue was most common (McCartney 2026).
- Achilles: a GP review lists Achilles rupture among expected squash injuries (Verow 1989). The only cohort data I found are badminton: mean age about 36, sudden forward push-off, mostly mid or late game, which suggests fatigue (Kaalund 1989; Fahlström 1998). Low back: no squash-specific study found.

## 4. Fixes summary (fault → capacity → exercise)

| Fault | Capacity | Exercise | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Slow jab, weak lead-leg drive | Lead-leg RFD | Plyometrics or jump rope, 8 wk, 3×/wk, 30 min | study-backed (Chottidao 2022, juniors) |
| Weak hooks | Rotational power | Rotation-oriented S&C, 8 wk | study-backed (Pinto 2026) |
| Poor uppercut sequencing | Joint angular velocity and coordination | Joint-specific velocity / coordination programme, 24 sessions | study-backed (Tajibaev 2026) |
| Acute punch-speed prep | PAPE | Dumbbell throw at ~8% of 5RM bench, punch 8–12 min later (rear-hand peak 9.81 m/s by StrikeTec; the sensor's validity was not checked) | study-backed, single study |
| Slow roundhouse | Kicking speed | Elastic-resistance kicking in warm-up (+3.3%) | study-backed (Aandahl 2018) |
| Slow lunge | Time to peak force | Ballistic unilateral lower-body work | study-backed predictor (Cronin 2003); the training effect itself is not tested |
| Arm-only punch, flat pivot, rounded-back shot, knee collapse in lunge | Trunk rotation, hip mobility, trunk stiffness, hip abductor strength | Chops, med-ball rotations, hinge patterning, split squats, lateral band work | coaching practice |

## 5. What one smartphone can measure

| Marker | 2D side-on | 2D front-on | MediaPipe 3D world landmarks | Expected accuracy / evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Knee and hip flexion (lunge, shot level change, support leg) | Yes | No | Yes (worse than 2D sagittal for these) | Single side-view MediaPipe BlazePose Heavy, knee: squat ICC 0.773 **only after** a confidence gate (≥ 0.3); 15.7% of dynamic observations failed it; with no gate, squat RMSE was 25.4° (Yue 2026). Two-iPad markerless squat/lunge/hinge: knee/hip flexion cross-correlation > 0.95, knee RMSE 6.4–12.3°, hip 11.3–17.4° (Liu 2025/2026). Learnings file already holds 3.5–5° for hip/knee in good conditions (IEEE Sensors 2024) |
| Ankle angle (kick plantarflexion, heel strike) | Weak | No | Weak | Ankle RMSE 21.5–25.7° (Liu 2025/2026). Pose models struggle with small ankle motion (Menychtas 2023). **Do not score** |
| Frontal plane: knee valgus/varus, hip adduction, stance width | — | Yes for distances; weak for angles | Weak | Hip adduction, knee varus and rotation cross-correlation < 0.49 (Liu 2025/2026). Remote 2D lateral lunge (frontal plane) failed the agreement threshold (Harrington 2023). Stance width as an ankle-distance ratio is probably fine but **unvalidated** |
| Trunk and pelvis rotation, hip–shoulder separation, kinematic sequence | No | Partial | Yes in principle | Transverse plane is the weakest everywhere: even a multi-camera markerless system had transverse RMSE up to 56.6° (Edwards 2025). **Single-phone rotation values are not trustworthy** |
| Fist and foot velocity | Only at high fps | — | — | **No markerless validation of fist velocity found.** Arithmetic: at 7 m/s a fist moves about 23 cm between frames at 30 fps, or about 3 cm at 240 fps, so 30 fps cannot resolve peak velocity and motion blur breaks the landmark. Kickboxing 2D side-view pose models: phase timing error ~0.04 s, femur angle ~7°; acceptable agreement only for a small subset of 17 features (Duvelek 2026; MediaPipe was not among the models tested) |
| Timing (shot duration, lunge contact time, split-step) | Yes | Yes | Yes | Phase-duration median error 0.043 s for the best model in kickboxing (Duvelek 2026). Usable for coarse timing, not for millisecond gaps |
| Wrist, forearm rotation, racket face | No | No | No | Needs IMU/EM sensors (Gatt 2021; Williams 2021). Glove/racket IMUs validated (Menzel & Potthast 2021: R² 0.97–0.99 for punch force, velocity and time vs Kistler/Vicon) |

**Practical rules from this evidence:**
1. Use side-on for sagittal angles (knee, hip, trunk lean). Gate every frame on landmark confidence and reject the recording if too many frames fail.
2. Show front-on outputs as distances and ratios (stance width, knee-over-foot line), not as angles.
3. Don't output fist/foot speed, rotation angles, ankle angles or kinematic-sequence timing as numbers. At most show them as a qualitative "watch this" flag.
4. Fast strikes need 120–240 fps slow-motion capture. Even then, no validation exists.

## 6. Gaps (no study found in this search)

- Hip–shoulder separation in punching; stance width norms; support-foot pivot angle in kicks.
- Sprawl biomechanics; level-change hip height norms; shot back-posture kinematics.
- Squash lunge kinematics (knee over toe, stride as % of leg length, trunk flexion during the lunge), split-step timing, T-recovery timing, and squash-specific low-back or Achilles mechanisms.
- Any prospective study linking a camera-visible fault to injury in these sports.
- Markerless validation for punching or for the squash lunge specifically.
